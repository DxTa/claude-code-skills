# PMO operating model

This is a governance design aid, not a claim to hold organizational authority.

## Inputs and context

Collect strategy, active work, capacity, decision rights, delivery maturity, current reporting cost, executive sponsor, team interfaces, portfolio outcomes, and the problem the PMO is meant to solve.

## Workflow

1. State the PMO's purpose as decisions or capacity it will improve, not reports it will collect.
2. Define what it owns—portfolio intake, prioritization, visibility, gates, dependencies, or benefits—and what delivery teams retain.
3. Set minimum evidence for gates, a real stop/pause path, escalation rights, and a low-cost reporting cadence.
4. Establish independence from the functions being governed and publish decisions, rationale, and exceptions.
5. Measure value through decision latency, work-in-progress, delivery reliability, capacity visibility, adoption, or realized benefits; remove rituals that change nothing.

## Output and decision record

Return PMO charter, decision-rights map, gate criteria, reporting contract, escalation route, value baseline and targets, risks, and review date.

## Uncertainty and failure handling

If authority, sponsorship, or capacity data is unclear, propose a bounded pilot and explicit assumptions. Do not infer that compliance or attendance means the PMO is useful; validate decisions and outcomes.

## Routing boundaries

Use this skill only for its stated capability and required context. For adjacent work, select a more specific skill from `skill-index.tsv`; do not infer coverage from this department folder or use this skill to authorize actions outside its scope.

## Never

- Collect status that feeds no decision.
- Take a delivery decision owned by the team doing the work.
- Create a gate that cannot pause, redirect, or stop work.
