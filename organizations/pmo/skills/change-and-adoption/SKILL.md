# Change and adoption

A delivered change has no realized value until affected people can and do use it.

## Inputs and context

Collect the change, affected groups, current and desired behaviors, lost benefits or friction, local influencers, readiness, communication channels, training/support capacity, policy constraints, and adoption baseline.

## Workflow

1. Map each audience's workflow, incentive, loss, decision power, and likely barrier; do not label disagreement as ignorance.
2. State the reason, audience-specific impact, required behavior, support, and timing in that order.
3. Involve credible practitioners early enough to change the design, not only endorse it.
4. Provide task-level guidance and support near the moment of use; capture workarounds and unresolved blockers.
5. Measure behavior and outcomes against baseline, including old-path use, completion, quality, and benefit indicators.

## Output and decision record

Return stakeholder/barrier map, rollout sequence, messages, enablement plan, adoption metric definitions and targets, feedback loop, risks, owners, and decision dates.

## Uncertainty and failure handling

Separate unwillingness, inability, missing access, poor design, and conflicting incentives. If a rollout affects employment, safety, privacy, or regulated operations, route policy interpretation and approval to the relevant qualified owner.

## Routing boundaries

Use this skill only for its stated capability and required context. For adjacent work, select a more specific skill from `skill-index.tsv`; do not infer coverage from this department folder or use this skill to authorize actions outside its scope.

## Never

- Report deployment or license counts as adoption.
- Involve affected experts only after decisions are final.
- Promise a change has no cost when its costs are visible.
