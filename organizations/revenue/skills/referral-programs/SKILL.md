# Referral programs

A referral program tests whether existing customer value can produce qualified, retained demand; incentives alone are not evidence.

## Inputs and context

Collect unprompted referral evidence, customer segment, success moment, economics, incentive options, attribution rules, payout timing, fraud signals, channel constraints, and baseline retention/conversion.

## Workflow

1. State the referral hypothesis and verify whether customers already recommend the product without incentives.
2. Put a simple, editable share action near a genuine success moment and give the referred person a relevant entry path.
3. Reward a qualifying activation or retained outcome, not merely a signup; define caps, hold periods, duplicate checks, and review ownership.
4. For affiliates, document attribution window, eligible actions, prohibited methods, and commission terms separately from customer referrals.
5. Compare referred acquisition cost, activation, retention, fraud, and margin against non-referred baseline with a target and review date.

## Output and decision record

Return program design, evidence versus hypothesis, incentive and fraud model, attribution rules, baseline and measurable targets, experiment result, and continue/change/stop decision.

## Uncertainty and failure handling

Do not attribute a customer without a clear rule. Investigate unusual clusters or self-referral signals before payout, and mark early signups as unproven until retention data matures.

## Never

- Assume incentives create authentic word of mouth.
- Pay for a signup when the desired outcome is a retained customer.
- Apply customer-referral rules to affiliates without commercial terms.
