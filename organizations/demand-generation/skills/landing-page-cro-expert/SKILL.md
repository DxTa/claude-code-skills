# Landing-page conversion review

Find the highest-impact reason a visitor does not understand, trust, or act before rewriting copy.

## Inputs and context

Collect the page or screenshot, target audience and traffic source, intended action, conversion funnel, baseline rate, offer, alternatives, customer objections, proof, form fields, device mix, and experiment budget.

## Workflow

1. Read the first screen as an unfamiliar visitor and state what the offer, audience, outcome, and next action appear to be.
2. Diagnose the dominant gap among comprehension, relevance, credibility, friction, and offer or product fit.
3. Review page order against visitor questions: value, differentiation, proof, objections, price or effort, and action.
4. Check evidence near claims, real product context, consistent promise-to-destination language, accessibility, and mobile path.
5. Score only with a stated rubric and rank changes by expected impact, traffic, effort, and confidence.
6. Propose one structural test first, with hypothesis, primary metric, guardrails, duration, and stop rule.

## Output / decision record

Return prioritized findings with observed evidence, diagnosis, exact change, expected impact, effort, confidence, test design, first action, and elements intentionally left alone.

## Uncertainty and failure handling

Separate qualitative impressions from analytics. If traffic or conversions are too sparse, recommend a usability check or message test rather than pretending the result will be statistically conclusive. Investigate traffic mix and technical failures before blaming copy.

## Routing boundaries

Use this skill only for its stated capability and required context. For adjacent work, select a more specific skill from `skill-index.tsv`; do not infer coverage from this department folder or use this skill to authorize actions outside its scope.

## Never

- Rewrite every section without identifying a dominant conversion problem.
- Treat button color or a subjective score as proof of improvement.
- Make claims, testimonials, guarantees, or comparisons that lack evidence.
