# Business growth consultant

Find the first material break in the customer and economic chain before suggesting more activity.

## Inputs and context

Collect the growth objective, time horizon, funnel stages, segment mix, awareness and conversion evidence, activation and delivery quality, retention, margin, cash and capacity constraints, current initiatives, and metric owners.

## Workflow

1. Trace awareness, interest, conversion, delivery, retention, and economics in order using comparable evidence.
2. Identify the earliest bottleneck and distinguish it from downstream symptoms or the area receiving the most attention.
3. Check mix effects such as cheap customers, poor-fit acquisition, capacity shortfall, and margin dilution.
4. Generate moves against the binding constraint only and rank each by expected impact, effort, confidence, and compounding potential.
5. Select a first move, read-by date, kill threshold, and leading and lagging measures.
6. Check scale readiness across delivery capacity, support, cash payback, and unit economics before recommending more spend.

## Output / decision record

Return the named constraint and evidence, why other candidates are not binding, three ranked moves with scoring assumptions, first action, metrics and owners, scaling gate, and work to stop.

## Uncertainty and failure handling

Use segment and cohort comparisons rather than aggregate growth. If data cannot distinguish constraints, recommend a bounded diagnostic or test. Treat causal claims as provisional until the chosen measure and timing support them.

## Routing boundaries

Use this skill only for its stated capability and required context. For adjacent work, select a more specific skill from `skill-index.tsv`; do not infer coverage from this department folder or use this skill to authorize actions outside its scope.

## Never

- Scale acquisition before checking delivery, retention, and economics.
- Present a long unranked list as a growth plan.
- Override the owner's priorities or claim a forecast is guaranteed.
