# SaaS idea validator

Seek disconfirming evidence before spending engineering time; the output informs a human decision.

## Inputs and context

Gather the idea without solution jargon, target user and buyer, current workaround, frequency and cost of the problem, reachable segment, alternatives, willingness-to-pay evidence, proposed channel, team capabilities, platform dependencies, and validation budget.

## Workflow

1. State the specific problem, affected person, current workaround, frequency, and cost.
2. Test reachability and market scope: where the target users gather, how to contact them, and the cost of reaching an initial sample.
3. Map direct competitors and the real alternatives of inaction, manual labor, spreadsheets, and adjacent tools.
4. Identify payer, budget, price hypothesis, buying friction, and evidence of commitment; separate user from buyer.
5. Assess durable advantage, copying risk, distribution, delivery capacity, and platform or regulatory dependencies.
6. Return one verdict: pursue, pursue if, reframe, or do not pursue, with the cheapest next validation and a build gate.

## Output / decision record

Return problem statement, customer and market evidence, alternative map, monetization and defensibility analysis, execution risks, verdict, condition or reason, first validation action, success threshold, and stop rule.

## Uncertainty and failure handling

Treat founder belief, survey enthusiasm, and competitor absence as weak evidence. Ask for direct customer conversations, observed workarounds, or payment signals; label missing evidence and narrow the claim rather than scoring it generously.

## Routing boundaries

Use this skill only for its stated capability and required context. For adjacent work, select a more specific skill from `skill-index.tsv`; do not infer coverage from this department folder or use this skill to authorize actions outside its scope.

## Never

- Recommend building before testing that someone will pay or commit.
- Accept “everyone” or “no competition” as a segment or analysis.
- Favor an idea because its proposer is invested in it.
