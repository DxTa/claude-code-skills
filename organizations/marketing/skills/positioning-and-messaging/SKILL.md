# Positioning and messaging

## Inputs and context

Gather customer research, current alternatives, product capabilities, differentiated attributes, urgent segments, category expectations, competitor evidence, approved proof, objections, brand voice, and legal or regulatory constraints. Mark assumptions that have not been tested with the target audience.

## Workflow

1. Describe what the priority buyer does today instead of using the product, including manual workarounds and competing options.
2. List factual differences that are available and supportable. Translate each into a customer consequence rather than a feature label.
3. Select the segment for whom the consequence matters now and define the category or frame that sets the right expectations.
4. Write one durable statement, a small set of value pillars with proof, objection responses, and terms to avoid.
5. Test the statement with target users: ask what they think the product is, who it serves, and what alternative it replaces.
6. Publish a versioned message of record and explain how each channel may change format without changing meaning.

## Output / decision record

Return alternative, attributes, customer value, priority segment, market frame, one-liner, proof-backed pillars, objections, prohibited or risky terms, test evidence, assumptions, approver, version, and next review trigger.

## Uncertainty and failure handling

If users name the wrong category, use case, or competitor, treat that as evidence of a framing problem rather than a copy-edit request. Keep unvalidated differentiation provisional. If proof is missing, narrow the claim or schedule research before asking campaigns to amplify it.

## Never

- Never claim superiority, uniqueness, or competitor comparison without support.
- Never widen the audience just to make the positioning sound larger.
- Never change the substantive promise by channel or hide material limitations.
