# Partnership marketing

## Inputs and context

Gather the objective, proposed partner, audience evidence, overlap and conflicts, offer, deliverables, dates, compensation, disclosure rules, data-sharing needs, asset ownership, approvals, attribution method, and each party’s fallback if work is late or incomplete.

## Workflow

1. Verify audience fit from actual content, engagement quality, and buyer relevance rather than reach claims alone.
2. Check trust, brand alignment, conflicts, accessibility, platform rules, and whether the partner receives fair value.
3. Write a working agreement covering deliverables, review rights, timing, disclosure, compensation, leads or revenue measurement, data permissions, asset ownership, and non-performance handling.
4. For creators, pay for defined work, preserve authentic editorial control, and label sponsored or affiliate relationships conspicuously.
5. Launch with consent-aware tracking and named follow-up owners. For communities, define member value, norms, moderation, and sustainable presence before growth goals.
6. Review direct, assisted, qualitative, and downstream outcomes against the pre-agreed method.

## Output / decision record

Return partner recommendation, audience evidence, mutual value, scope and deliverable table, commercial and disclosure terms, data/consent design, owners, risks and fallback, attribution assumptions, success criteria, and renewal or stop decision.

## Uncertainty and failure handling

Assume follower and performance figures may be incomplete or inflated until verified. Separate partner-sourced influence from last-touch conversion. If legal, platform, disclosure, audience, or data terms are unresolved, do not launch; document the dependency and decision owner.

## Never

- Never promote through a partner whose audience or incentives have not been examined.
- Never hide sponsorship, affiliate compensation, or material limitations.
- Never exchange contact data without a lawful purpose, consent, and agreed controls.
