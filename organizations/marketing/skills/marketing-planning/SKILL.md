# Marketing planning

## Inputs and context

Collect the primary objective and deadline, evidence about the current growth constraint, segment priorities, historical channel performance, fixed and variable costs, budget ceiling, capabilities, dependencies, approved positioning, and measurement/privacy rules.

## Workflow

1. Select the dominant constraint: discovery, qualified reach, belief, action, or retention. Cite the evidence and list competing interpretations.
2. Set one primary target and supporting indicators. Define segments in priority order and name who is out of scope.
3. Classify channels as established, promising, or experimental based on evidence and capacity. Cap experiments at a loss the business can absorb.
4. Sequence work around prerequisites such as positioning, content, product readiness, approvals, and fulfillment.
5. Allocate fixed and variable budget, including the first cut if the plan must shrink.
6. Assign a leading indicator and read-by date to every material investment. Set kill, revise, and expand thresholds.
7. Pressure-test dependency failure, half-budget delivery, and downstream customer quality before approval.

## Output / decision record

Return objective/date, chosen constraint and evidence, segment priority, channel classification and rationale, sequence, budget allocation, dependencies, measures and read-by dates, kill/expand criteria, non-funded work, approvers, and review cadence.

## Uncertainty and failure handling

Separate historical results, forecasts, and causal claims. State attribution limits and confidence ranges. If the constraint cannot be distinguished, fund a bounded diagnostic rather than spreading budget across every possibility. Re-plan when assumptions or platform rules change.

## Never

- Never allocate budget without a measurable objective, owner, and review date.
- Never report activity or traffic as success when the plan requires qualified outcomes.
- Never fund an experiment whose failure would threaten required operations.
