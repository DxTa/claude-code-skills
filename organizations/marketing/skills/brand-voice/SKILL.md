# Brand voice

## Inputs and context

Gather representative writing samples with permission, the intended audience, channels, content goals, accessibility needs, approved terminology, and words or claims that require review. If samples are unavailable, gather explicit choices about tone, stance, formality, humor, rhythm, and topics to avoid.

## Workflow

1. Confirm whose voice is being represented, who approves it, and where the guidance applies.
2. Select varied samples rather than a single memorable piece. Separate durable patterns from topic-specific language.
3. Describe observable choices: sentence shape, vocabulary, openings, level of certainty, point of view, humor, formatting, and use of evidence.
4. Turn each pattern into a practical rule with a positive example and a counterexample created for the project.
5. Add a prohibited or high-risk language list, including unsupported promises and sensitive claims.
6. Draft a short test passage, request owner feedback, and revise the rules when feedback reveals a repeatable preference.

## Output / decision record

Return a voice guide containing scope, audience, rules, examples, counterexamples, do-not-use terms, approved claims, open questions, owner, and review date. Record whether each rule came from a sample, an explicit choice, or an unresolved hypothesis. If requested, provide a separate background brief only from facts the person or brand approved.

## Uncertainty and failure handling

Do not treat adjectives such as “friendly” or “bold” as sufficient evidence. Mark patterns with too few samples as provisional and ask for more examples. Keep voice decisions separate from factual, legal, accessibility, and brand-identity approvals. If the requested voice conflicts with an approved identity or audience need, surface the conflict rather than silently choosing.

## Routing boundaries

Use this skill only for its stated capability and required context. For adjacent work, select a more specific skill from `skill-index.tsv`; do not infer coverage from this department folder or use this skill to authorize actions outside its scope.

## Never

- Never impersonate a real person without authorization.
- Never invent first-hand stories, credentials, quotations, or personal opinions to make copy sound authentic.
- Never use the voice guide to bypass consent, accessibility, legal, or brand approvals.
