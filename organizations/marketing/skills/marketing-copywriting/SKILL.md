# Marketing copywriting

## Inputs and context

Require the reader and their current belief, one intended takeaway, one call to action, actual objection, approved positioning, customer language, available proof, channel limits, voice guidance, accessibility needs, and legal or brand review requirements.

## Workflow

1. Confirm the communication job and the single action. If the audience or objection is unclear, ask for research or mark assumptions.
2. Arrange the argument in the reader’s order: recognized problem, consequence, distinct approach, evidence, and next step.
3. Lead with an observable customer outcome and use specific, checkable language. Attach proof near every material claim.
4. Adapt the same approved meaning to the surface without changing the promise or hiding important conditions.
5. Edit in passes: structure, removal of nonessential language, specificity, read-aloud, accessibility, and claim review.
6. Deliver variants only when the test question and approval boundary are explicit.

## Output / decision record

Return the draft or edited copy, surface and audience, CTA, claim-to-evidence table, assumptions/placeholders, variants and test question, unresolved risks, accessibility notes, approver, and publication status.

## Uncertainty and failure handling

Use placeholders for missing numbers, testimonials, dates, or performance claims; never fill gaps with plausible inventions. Flag customer language that is anecdotal or privacy-sensitive. If proof does not support the promise, narrow the claim or route the issue to positioning, Product, Legal & Risk, or Customer Experience.

## Routing boundaries

Use this skill only for its stated capability and required context. For adjacent work, select a more specific skill from `skill-index.tsv`; do not infer coverage from this department folder or use this skill to authorize actions outside its scope.

## Never

- Never invent statistics, endorsements, customer quotations, guarantees, or comparative claims.
- Never conceal material terms or use copy to obstruct consent or cancellation.
- Never publish personal or sensitive audience information without documented permission.
