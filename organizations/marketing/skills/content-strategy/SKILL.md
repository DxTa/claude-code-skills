# Content strategy

## Inputs and context

Start with a business or customer outcome, priority audience, customer evidence, existing content, differentiated expertise, available formats and capacity, candidate platforms, distribution constraints, and measurement history. Record content rights, privacy, accessibility, and brand requirements.

## Workflow

1. Identify the narrow intersection of audience need, credible expertise, and a useful point of view. State the topics intentionally excluded.
2. Assign each content job to reach, trust, conversion, or retention, and choose a format that supports that job.
3. Select only the platforms the team can sustain and explain the audience and format evidence for each.
4. Set a realistic cadence, source piece, derivatives, owners, and review points.
5. Choose the first set of pieces, each with one audience problem, one job, one evidence plan, and one success measure.
6. Define the threshold and date for dropping, revising, or expanding a topic.

## Output / decision record

Return the territory, priority audience, excluded audience or topics, four-job allocation, platform rationale, cadence, initial content slate, derivative plan, measures by job, review dates, and stop criteria.

## Uncertainty and failure handling

Treat audience demand and channel fit as hypotheses until supported by research or observed behavior. Do not compare unlike outcomes as if they were equivalent. If evidence is missing, propose a small learning cycle and cap the commitment. Revisit the strategy when performance, customer needs, or platform rules change.

## Routing boundaries

Use `chief-content-officer` for production systems and editorial operations. Use `marketing-campaign-planner` for one bounded campaign. Do not use this skill as a substitute for customer research or positioning decisions.

## Never

- Never use traffic as the sole measure for every content job.
- Never promise a cadence that exceeds sustainable capacity.
- Never make unsupported expertise, outcome, or customer claims.
