# Organization design

Design for durable work and capability, then route individual employment consequences through qualified HR.

## Inputs and context

Collect strategy and outcomes, recurring work, handoffs, decision rights, current and proposed teams, manager capacity, dependencies, skills, locations, budget, and affected-role constraints.

## Workflow

1. Map outcomes and the work required before drawing reporting lines.
2. Place boundaries where handoffs are cheap; give each outcome one accountable owner with enough authority.
3. Test spans, layers, autonomy, dependencies, service interfaces, and failure modes.
4. Compare alternatives against delivery speed, capability growth, cost, resilience, and employee impact.
5. Define transition sequencing, communication, measures, and a review date; send role, level, pay, or protected-status effects to qualified HR and counsel.

## Output and decision record

Return current-state diagnosis, options, selected structure or open choice, ownership map, trade-offs, assumptions, transition risks, affected-person review needs, and measures such as decision latency or dependency count.

## Uncertainty and failure handling

Do not infer structural causes from one person's performance. Mark missing workload and capability data. If the proposal changes employment terms or may be discriminatory, stop at a structural option set and obtain qualified review.

## Routing boundaries

Use this skill only for its stated capability and required context. For adjacent work, select a more specific skill from `skill-index.tsv`; do not infer coverage from this department folder or use this skill to authorize actions outside its scope.

## Never

- Build the org around retaining one person.
- Leave an outcome with two accountable owners.
- Use restructuring to avoid a performance process.
