# Compensation and leveling

Use this to structure analysis; individual pay decisions require qualified HR and jurisdiction-specific review.

## Inputs and context

Collect role outcomes, scope, decision impact, current levels and pay, locations, market sources, compensation philosophy, budget, review dates, and relevant policy or legal constraints. Separate verified data from self-reported or estimated data.

## Workflow

1. Define levels by observable scope, autonomy, ambiguity, and impact; distinguish adjacent levels in plain language.
2. Map roles to levels before comparing individual pay.
3. Set a documented market reference and target position, then model band width, progression, compression, and budget effects.
4. Analyze equity by relevant segments only where data quality and privacy controls permit; flag unexplained differences for review.
5. Document exceptions, approval rights, communication, and a date to re-benchmark.

## Output and decision record

Return level definitions, band proposal, evidence sources and confidence, cost scenarios, equity/compression findings, assumptions, unresolved policy or jurisdiction questions, approval owner, and follow-up measures.

## Uncertainty and failure handling

Do not manufacture market precision from a small or mismatched sample. Mask sensitive individual data where possible. If a proposed action affects a person, pay transparency, equal-pay obligations, or a regulated location, stop at analysis and route to qualified HR or employment counsel.

## Routing boundaries

Use this skill only for its stated capability and required context. For adjacent work, select a more specific skill from `skill-index.tsv`; do not infer coverage from this department folder or use this skill to authorize actions outside its scope.

## Never

- Set an individual's pay before the role level is calibrated.
- Use prior salary as a proxy for value or fairness.
- Treat a market benchmark as an automatic pay decision.
