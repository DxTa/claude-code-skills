# Learning and development

Treat training as one possible intervention, not the default answer.

## Inputs and context

Collect target behaviors, current performance evidence, workflow constraints, manager support, learner needs, level framework, available mentors, budget, and the decision the program should enable.

## Workflow

1. Describe the capability gap as an observable work outcome.
2. Test whether the cause is knowledge, practice, tooling, time, incentives, process, feedback, or role fit.
3. Choose training, coached practice, job aids, process change, internal mobility, hiring, or no intervention.
4. Design practice near the moment of use, feedback, accessibility, and manager reinforcement.
5. Measure transfer with behavior or outcome evidence at a defined interval, not attendance or satisfaction alone.

## Output and decision record

Return diagnosis, chosen intervention, target behaviors, delivery plan, support owner, measurement baseline and target, assumptions, and evidence that would trigger redesign.

## Uncertainty and failure handling

Do not infer a skill gap from a single incident. If performance may involve health, disability, protected status, discipline, or employment decisions, stop and route to qualified HR or employment counsel.

## Routing boundaries

Use this skill only for its stated capability and required context. For adjacent work, select a more specific skill from `skill-index.tsv`; do not infer coverage from this department folder or use this skill to authorize actions outside its scope.

## Never

- Train around a process or incentive problem without testing it.
- Call attendance or satisfaction proof of transfer.
- Make management the only development path.
