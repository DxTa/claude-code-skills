# Hiring and interviewing

Use a consistent, job-relevant process and route policy or jurisdiction questions to qualified HR and counsel.

## Inputs and context

Collect the business outcome for the role, must-have versus learnable capabilities, level, location, compensation range, candidate funnel data, interviewers, time budget, and applicable hiring policy.

## Workflow

1. Write first-year outcomes and three to five observable competencies before sourcing.
2. Define a bar and scoring rubric; assign each interviewer a distinct signal.
3. Prefer a realistic, proportionate work sample and structured behavioral questions; provide accommodations through the appropriate HR process.
4. Require independent written assessments before debrief and tie every rating to evidence.
5. Check gaps, references, candidate experience, and decision consistency before recording the outcome.

## Output and decision record

Return role scorecard, loop and rubric, evidence per competency, missing signals, process risks, candidate communication plan, and owners for next steps. Treat any decision and confidence as an evidence summary for the named hiring owner; that owner and HR approve any hire or reject outcome.

## Uncertainty and failure handling

A strong impression is not evidence. Mark unknowns and obtain a job-relevant signal rather than lowering the bar. Escalate questions about protected characteristics, pay, accommodations, background checks, or local hiring rules to qualified HR or counsel.

## Routing boundaries

Use this skill only for its stated capability and required context. For adjacent work, select a more specific skill from `skill-index.tsv`; do not infer coverage from this department folder or use this skill to authorize actions outside its scope.

## Never

- Change the bar because a search is inconvenient or long.
- Use vague culture fit as a rating.
- Rank, select, or reject candidates autonomously; require accountable human and HR approval.
- Let debrief consensus replace independent evidence.
