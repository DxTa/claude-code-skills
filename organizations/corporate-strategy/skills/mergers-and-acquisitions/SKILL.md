# Mergers and acquisitions

This skill organizes commercial reasoning; qualified legal, tax, accounting, and technical advisers must make specialist determinations.

## Inputs and context

Gather the pre-written strategic thesis, target materials, customer and financial evidence, proposed consideration, constraints, decision gates, and integration owner. Record what is known directly from the target versus independently verified.

## Workflow

1. Define the capability, market access, or economics the transaction is meant to obtain and why alternatives are insufficient.
2. Screen the target against that thesis, including customer concentration, retention, people dependency, operating fit, liabilities, and undocumented systems or contracts.
3. Build valuation cases with and without synergies; distinguish realizable cost effects from uncertain revenue effects.
4. Set a walk-away boundary and list diligence findings that would invalidate the thesis.
5. Design the first 30/60/100-day integration choices: what combines, what stays separate, ownership, customer continuity, and talent retention.
6. Present a gated recommendation for proceed, pause, renegotiate, or stop.

## Output / decision record

Return a thesis test, target scorecard, verified and unverified findings, valuation range with assumptions, walk-away condition, integration risks, decision gates, specialist workstreams, and named next actions.

## Uncertainty and failure handling

Mark management claims as unverified until supported by source records or independent checks. Use ranges and sensitivity cases rather than false precision. Pause when diligence cannot test a material assumption, and route legal, tax, accounting, privacy, employment, and technical findings to qualified reviewers.

## Routing boundaries

Use this skill only for its stated capability and required context. For adjacent work, select a more specific skill from `skill-index.tsv`; do not infer coverage from this department folder or use this skill to authorize actions outside its scope.

## Never

- Rebuild the thesis to fit an attractive available target.
- Treat a signed transaction as proof of value or as the end of the work.
- Declare legal, tax, accounting, or employment conclusions without specialists.
