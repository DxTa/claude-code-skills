# Scenario planning

Use scenarios to prepare choices, not to pretend to predict one future.

## Inputs and context

Capture the decision, horizon, current plan, fixed constraints, known facts, assumptions, dependencies, and signals that can be observed over time. Include the people who can act when a trigger fires.

## Workflow

1. List assumptions and separate predetermined conditions from uncertain variables.
2. Select the two or three uncertainties that would materially change the decision.
3. Construct three or four internally consistent futures from those variables; give each a memorable logic-based name.
4. Stress-test the current plan: identify what remains useful, what breaks first, and what should have started earlier.
5. Classify actions as robust moves, contingent preparations, or low-cost options.
6. Assign an owner, measurement method, threshold, and review cadence to each early-warning indicator.

## Output / decision record

Return the decision and horizon, assumptions, scenarios, stress-test table, robust actions, contingent actions, options, indicators with thresholds and owners, and the next review trigger.

## Uncertainty and failure handling

Do not assign unsupported probabilities. If an uncertainty cannot be observed, record that limitation and use a reversible option or explicit human judgment. Revisit the work when a trigger changes the facts; do not quietly treat an old scenario as current.

## Routing boundaries

Use this skill only for its stated capability and required context. For adjacent work, select a more specific skill from `skill-index.tsv`; do not infer coverage from this department folder or use this skill to authorize actions outside its scope.

## Never

- Rename one forecast as optimistic, base, and pessimistic scenarios.
- End with scenarios but no action for the current week.
- Treat a scenario as a forecast or commitment.
