# Portfolio strategy

Make allocation choices from comparable evidence rather than inertia, advocacy, or absolute size.

## Inputs and context

Collect the candidate lines, common time period, investment requests, returns and risks, market evidence, strategic capabilities, customer obligations, and decision constraints. Set scoring criteria and weights before reviewing the line-specific cases.

## Workflow

1. Define the portfolio objective and the scarce resource being allocated.
2. Assess each line's market attractiveness and the organization's evidence-backed ability to win.
3. Compare the marginal result of the next unit of investment, not only current scale or profit.
4. Expose deliberate and accidental cross-subsidies, dependencies, and exit obligations.
5. Force a ranked list with fund, maintain, harvest, pause, or exit postures.
6. Set a trigger and review date for every conditional call.

## Output / decision record

Return the criteria and weights, comparable scorecard, ranked posture for each line, proposed allocation, explicit stops or exits, tradeoffs, evidence gaps, and reversal indicators with owners.

## Uncertainty and failure handling

Normalize unlike metrics and disclose incomparable data. Show sensitivity to disputed assumptions and keep a line conditional when its case depends on missing evidence. Before an exit, confirm customer, employee, contractual, and operational obligations with the responsible owners.

## Never

- Keep every initiative funded by calling all of them priorities.
- Use sunk cost or internal advocacy as prospective return.
- Recommend an exit without an executable obligation and transition check.
